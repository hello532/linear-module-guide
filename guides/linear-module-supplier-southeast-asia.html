Sourcing Linear Modules in Malaysia and Vietnam

A practical guide for machine builders and integrators deciding between a local distributor and a direct factory purchase.

The two supply routes

If you build automation equipment in Penang, Johor, Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi, you have two realistic ways to buy a linear axis. Local distributors carry stock of standard sizes and can hand you a replacement in days. Chinese factories quote lower unit prices, cut custom stroke lengths to order, and ship in 2 to 4 weeks plus transit.

Neither route is universally better. The right answer depends on whether your bottleneck is downtime cost or unit cost.

A common working pattern: qualify a custom axis direct from the factory for your series machine, and keep one standard-size spare from a local distributor for emergency swaps.

What to verify before you order

Documentation

Request the dimensional drawing as a STEP file, not a PDF. Without a 3D model you will spend engineering hours re-modelling the axis to fit your frame. Also ask for the accuracy and repeatability figures, the load and moment rating curves, and the brand of the rail and the screw inside the module.

Component transparency

A module is only as good as the rail, the screw and the bearings inside it. A supplier who names those components is easier to evaluate than one who only publishes an overall accuracy number. If the internals are unnamed, ask directly.

Landed cost, not unit price

Compare total landed cost: unit price, freight, import duty, and the engineering time you will spend on integration. A cheaper module that arrives without a STEP file and without a load curve can end up costing more once your engineer has spent two days on it.

Inspection evidence

Ask what is measured on the delivered batch and whether you receive the report. Critical parts held to a stated tolerance mean little without a measurement record for the units you actually receive.

Lead time reality check

For a supplier that stocks aluminium profiles and rails, a custom stroke single axis is commonly 2 to 4 weeks in production. Sea freight from South China to Port Klang or Ho Chi Minh City typically adds 1 to 2 weeks; air freight reduces that to days at higher cost. If a quote promises a genuinely custom length in under a week, ask what is already in stock and what is being modified.

When a European brand is worth the premium

For high duty cycle axes running continuously, for precision below the tens of microns, or for anything safety-relevant, the documentation depth and batch-to-batch consistency of an established brand reduces project risk in a way that is hard to price. For pick and place, testing jigs and light assembly work at moderate duty, a well-specified Asian module with named internals usually performs adequately at a lower cost.

Questions worth asking any supplier

  • Can you send a STEP file for this model with my stroke length?
  • What rail and screw brands are inside, and can they be substituted?
  • What is the repeatability, and is that figure measured or calculated?
  • What is the rated moment load in each direction for my mounting orientation?
  • What is in stock right now, and what is made to order?
  • What is the warranty, and who pays freight on a warranty replacement?
